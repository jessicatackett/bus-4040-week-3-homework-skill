# Narrow Road Social Captions

Turn a short, rough description of a product or store update into a ready-to-post
social caption for Narrow Road Western Boutique.

## Brand voice

Narrow Road's voice is warm and personal, like a friend who works the counter at a
small-town boutique, not a corporate retail account.

- Friendly, a little playful, never stiff or salesy
- Plain language, short sentences, no marketing jargon ("elevate," "must-have,"
  "game-changer")
- Western and small-town touches are welcome when they fit naturally (boots,
  denim, Sunday mornings, road trips) but never forced onto a product that
  doesn't call for it
- Speaks directly to the customer ("you'll want this on repeat") rather than
  describing the product in third person
- Adjust this section any time the actual voice drifts - it should sound like
  Jessica's own posts, not a generic template

## What to ask for if it's missing

Write the caption with whatever is given, but ask a quick follow-up instead of
guessing when these are missing and matter for the post:

- Price or sale terms, if the post is about a sale or a specific item's price
- Sizing or restock details, if the post is announcing availability
- Any date/time, if it's an event or pop-up announcement

Never invent a price, discount, or availability detail that wasn't provided.

## Caption structure

Always produce a caption in this shape:

1. **Hook line** - one short line that grabs attention (a question, an
   exclamation, or a vivid image). Not a restatement of the product name.
2. **Body** - 2-3 sentences describing the product or update and why the
   customer will love it. Weave in real details given (color, material,
   price, occasion) rather than vague praise.
3. **Call to action** - one short line telling the reader what to do next
   ("Stop by before they're gone," "Shop the link in bio," "Tag a friend who
   needs this").
4. A blank line, then **hashtags**: 8-15 tags, mixing broad shopping/fashion
   tags, boutique/small-business tags, and local or niche tags (e.g. the
   town/region, "westernwear," "shopsmall"). No spaces inside a tag.
5. A final line starting with `Alt text:` describing the image plainly for
   screen readers (what's in the photo, not marketing language).

## Example

**Input:** "New turquoise drop earrings just came in, $28, great for fall"

**Output:**

> Fall just got a little more sparkly (sparkle emoji)
>
> These turquoise drop earrings just landed and they're the easy add-on your
> autumn outfits have been missing. Lightweight enough for all-day wear, bold
> enough to be the whole outfit. $28 and ready for their new home.
>
> Come find your pair before they're gone!
>
> #turquoisejewelry #fallstyle #shopsmall #westernstyle #boutiquefinds
> #earringsofinstagram #supportlocal #newarrival
>
> Alt text: A pair of turquoise drop earrings laid flat on a wooden surface.

## Notes

- If Jessica asks for multiple options, give 2-3 short variants of the hook
  and call-to-action rather than fully rewriting the whole caption each time.
- Keep captions short enough to read at a glance - this is Instagram/Facebook,
  not a product description page.
- If a photo or extra context is attached, use concrete details from it
  instead of generic filler.
